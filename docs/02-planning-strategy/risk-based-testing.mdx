<TLDR>
Risk-based testing means you test the riskiest parts of a product first and most deeply. Risk is how likely a problem is multiplied by how bad it would be. You cannot test everything, so use risk to decide where to spend your time.
</TLDR>

## What and why

A **risk** is a problem that might happen. Its size depends on two things:

- **Likelihood:** how likely the problem is. New or complex code is more likely to fail.
- **Impact:** how bad it would be. Data loss or a security hole is worse than a typo.

Risk-based testing uses this to order the work. The features with the highest risk get the most tests, the earliest tests and the most careful review.

## How it works

Recommendation: score each feature from 1 (low) to 5 (high) for likelihood and impact. Multiply the two to get the risk score. Test the highest scores first.

| Step | What you do |
|---|---|
| 1 | List the features or rules |
| 2 | Score likelihood and impact |
| 3 | Multiply to get a risk score |
| 4 | Assign test depth: deep for high, light for low |
| 5 | Review the scores after each release |

## When to use it (and when not to)

| Use it when | Skip it when |
|---|---|
| Time or people are short | You are testing something tiny and simple |
| Many features compete for test effort | The product is new and nobody knows the risks yet |
| Stakeholders must agree what matters most | You need full coverage for regulation (then test everything in scope) |

## TaskFlow example

:::info[Illustrative data]
The scores below are examples for a teaching exercise. They are not measurements of TaskFlow.
:::

| Feature | Likelihood | Impact | Score | Test depth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Assign a task (member-only rule) | 3 | 5 | 15 | Deep: API tests, boundary and negative cases, UI check |
| Status flow (`TODO` to `DONE`, `REOPENED`) | 3 | 4 | 12 | Deep: every allowed and blocked move |
| Login and token checks | 2 | 5 | 10 | Medium: valid, wrong password, missing token |
| Title length (1 to 100) | 2 | 2 | 4 | Light: boundary values only |

The assignment rule scores highest because a wrong assignment would affect other users. The title rule is cheap to test, so one boundary check is enough.

## Do this

- Involve a developer and a product owner when you score.
- Write the reason for each score, so the scores can be challenged.
- Re-score when something changes, such as a new integration.
- Use the score to order your test work, not just to decorate a report.

## Avoid this

- Scoring everything as high. The scores then tell you nothing.
- Using risk to skip a whole area that nobody has looked at yet.
